# Glossary: AI Words at the Kitchen Table

Every AI word your kid (or their teacher, or their favorite app's settings page) will throw at you — explained the way you'd explain it to a friend. About ten words here do the real work; the rest are the ones you'll meet in headlines and menus.

## The words that matter most

**AI (artificial intelligence)** — Software that learned patterns from an enormous amount of human writing and other data, so it can predict, answer, write, and make things. Good at sounds-like-a-person; not a person.

**Chatbot** — An AI you talk to by typing (or talking). The chat window. Also the thing that can *feel* like a friend if you're not careful.

**Hallucination** — When AI confidently says something that's simply wrong or made up. Not lying on purpose — there's no "on purpose" there — but just as wrong as a lie. The reason we double-check important answers.

**Machine learning** — How AI got smart: instead of being programmed with rules, it learns patterns from thousands of examples. Teach a computer to spot cats by showing it a million cat photos.

**Large language model (LLM)** — The specific kind of AI inside chat assistants. Reads text, predicts what comes next. GPT-style tools are LLMs. "Language" is in the name because that's what it does — words in, words out.

**Prompt** — What you type to an AI. Kids old enough to use AI are old enough to learn that better prompts get better answers: "Explain this to a 10-year-old" beats "help."

**Training data** — The books, websites, and conversations AI learned from. If wrong or unfair stuff is in there, wrong and unfair stuff comes out. AI doesn't fact-check its sources; it *had no sources* — just patterns.

## The settings-page words

**Chat history** — The saved record of your conversation. Lives on the company's servers. Exists even if you close the tab. Find the setting to clear it.

**Memory** — Some chatbots remember facts about you *between* conversations ("you like Taylor Swift"). Comforting and creepy at the same time; find the toggle.

**Personalization** — The AI adjusting what it says based on what it knows about you. Sometimes helpful, sometimes it's just a mirror with an angle. Worth turning off for homework.

**Model** — The "brain" a specific AI product runs on. Different apps use different models; one product can offer several. Models have version numbers and different skills and gotchas.

**Voice mode** — Talking out loud with an AI instead of typing. Feels *extra* person-like. Same rules apply — even more so, because kids relax when talking.

**Image generator** — AI that makes pictures from a description. Where deepfakes and "fake sticker" mischief live. Also the place to teach: no real person's face, ever.

**Deepfake** — A fake photo, voice, or video made to look like a real person said or did something. New enough to be a standard school-policy word now.

## The homework and honesty words

**Cheating, AI version** — Submitting AI's work as yours when your teacher says that's not allowed. The *hiding* is what turns tech use into a discipline issue.

**Disclosure** — Telling your teacher how you used AI. One sentence, and the honest path gets easy.

**Citation / source** — Where a fact came from. AI invents these — real-looking quotes and links that don't exist — which is why "mention your sources" needs the addendum "verify the source exists first."

**Double-check** — What a family does with every AI answer that matters. The skill the whole manual is quietly teaching.

## The safety words

**Data privacy** — What happens to what you type, where it's stored, who can see it. Rule of the house: *if you typed it, treat it as published.*

**Parasocial relationship** — A one-way "friendship" with someone who doesn't know you back. Chatbots are engineered props for this; kids are wired to fall for it. The word for the thing in [When a Chatbot Feels Like a Friend](03-when-a-chatbot-feels-like-a-friend.html).

**Age verification / terms of service (ToS)** — The rules a tool states for who can use it, including minimum ages (often 13, sometimes 18). Reading the age lines once beats arguing about it monthly.

**Parental controls** — Device or app settings that limit what kids can do. Useful; never a replacement for the kitchen-table conversation.

**Report / flag button** — The in-app button that tells the company a conversation went somewhere bad. Show your kid where it is for every tool they use.

## Bigger words, once

**Agent** — An AI that *does things* rather than just talks: books a flight, drafts files, sends email. Coming to consumer apps fast; the same rules apply — an agent that acts like a person is still not a person.

**Algorithm** — The recipe a piece of software follows; when people blame "the algorithm" for bad recommendations, they mean the software choosing what to show next, usually to maximize attention.

**Open-source** — Software (or model weights) whose recipe is published, so anyone can inspect or run it. Word you'll see in headlines; useful to know that "open" does not mean "free of the same risks."

**API** — The plumbing that lets one app use another app's AI behind the scenes. Rarely matters at the kitchen table; it explains why a "calculator app" suddenly can chat.

**AGI (artificial general intelligence)** — The hypothetical future AI that can do almost anything a person can. Useful for one honest sentence: "Grown-ups argue about whether and when; nobody knows; the tools today are impressive and still just tools."
