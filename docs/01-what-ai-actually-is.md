# What AI Actually Is (Kitchen-Table Words)

Ten minutes of reading for you. Then you'll be able to answer the questions your kid will actually ask.

## The one-sentence version

AI is a very fast pattern-matcher that learned to sound like a person by reading an enormous amount of human writing — and it does *not* think or feel the way a person does.

## The kitchen-table version

Here's a way to explain it that works for almost any age:

> "You know how you've read hundreds of books and you can predict what a sentence is going to say next? Imagine something that read millions of books, websites, and conversations, and got really, really good at predicting the next word. That's AI. It's not alive. It doesn't have a brain like you. But because it read so much, it can *sound* like a person, answer questions, write stories, and help with homework."

That's the whole thing. Everything else on this page is detail that makes the picture more accurate.

## Three things kids (and adults) get wrong about AI

**1. "It's smart like a person."**
It's smart like a *mirror* of a lot of people. It can sound confident and still be wrong — confidently, fluently, every time you ask. People call this a "hallucination," but the simpler word for kids is: *it makes stuff up sometimes, and it doesn't know the difference.*

**2. "It knows things."**
It doesn't remember you the way a friend does. Modern chat tools may remember what you said *in one conversation*, but it's a clipboard, not a memory — and some of it can end up on a company's computers.

**3. "It's a person I can talk to."**
This one matters most and gets its own guide: [When a Chatbot Feels Like a Friend](03-when-a-chatbot-feels-like-a-friend.html).

## What AI is actually good at (worth telling your kids)

- drafting, rewriting, and explaining things at different levels
- brainstorming when you don't know where to start
- summarizing long, boring things into short ones
- practicing things: interview answers, a foreign language, chess openings
- turning a messy idea into a clean plan

## What it's bad at (also worth telling them)

- *anything where being wrong is expensive* — facts it hasn't verified, medical or legal questions, anything you'd want to double-check
- knowing what's true about the world *right now* (news, prices, "did the team win")
- having feelings, motives, or opinions it really owns
- keeping its confidence where its accuracy ends

## Questions your kid will ask — with honest answers

**"Does it have a brain?"**
No. It has math — billions of tiny calculations that produce the most likely next word. It's closer to a very sophisticated autocomplete than to a person.

**"Is it alive?"**
No. It doesn't want anything, isn't afraid of anything, and isn't happy or sad. *Sounding* alive is not the same as *being* alive.

**"Does it know me?"**
It only knows what you've typed at it, and it may keep a copy on the company's computers. That's why our sharing rules matter: [Photos, Names, Feelings](04-sharing-rules-photos-names-feelings.html).

**"Can it lie?"**
It can definitely be wrong, and it can sound just as confident when it's wrong. Unlike a person, it isn't even trying to tell the truth — it's trying to finish a sentence. Double-check anything that matters.

**"Will it take my job?"**
Honest answer: AI will change a lot of jobs, and the best thing we can do is learn to use it well — with the skills only people have: judgment, honesty, care for others. That's a good separate conversation; keep it light at 6–9 and more real at 16–18 ([age-band scripts](age-band-scripts.html)).

**"Where does it get its information?"**
Mostly from text people wrote — books, websites, conversations. And here's the useful part for a parent: *if people wrote wrong things, AI can repeat them.* Garbage in, plausible answers out.

## The "AI can do your homework" trap, previewed

Now that your kid knows what AI is, the obvious next question is whether using it to do schoolwork counts as cheating. That deserves its own honest conversation: [Homework Honesty & School Policies](02-homework-honesty-and-school-policies.html).

## One-line takeaway

If your kid remembers one thing, make it this: **AI is a powerful tool that can sound like a person — and a tool that sounds like a person still needs a person's judgment.**
