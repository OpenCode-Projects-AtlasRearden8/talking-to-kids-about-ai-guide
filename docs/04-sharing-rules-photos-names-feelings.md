# Sharing Rules: Photos, Names, Feelings

Everything your kid types or uploads to an AI tool leaves the house. Here's a family rule simple enough for a 7-year-old and true for a 17-year-old.

## The one rule that covers almost everything

**Don't give an AI anything you wouldn't put on a poster at school with your name on it.**

Not because an AI is evil — because a copy of it may sit on a company's servers, be read by an employee, be used for training, or exist long after your kid forgot they sent it. Ten-year-olds can understand "it doesn't come back once you send it." That's the whole rule.

## Photos and faces

Default: **no photos of people or places we know, unless a parent says yes first.** That includes the big three:

- **their own face** (and any other kid's face — yes, even "just for fun" cartoons of a classmate; some tools turn faces into deepfakes, which is its own trouble)
- **the outside of your house** (street number, house layout)
- **anything showing license plates, school names, or screens in the background**

Fine uploads: a drawing they made, a photo of their robot project, a plant they want identified, their @-handle-scraped-free pet sitting on their homework.

Also worth saying to teens: "reverse image search" and face-matching tools exist, and anything they upload today can be searchable far into the future.

## Names and identity details

Default: **first name and age only, and only if a tool requires it.** Skip:

- last names — theirs, their friends', your name
- exactly where the live (neighborhood, school, house)
- birth dates, addresses, real phone numbers
- school names on anything uploaded (team shirts in photos, the school yearbook PDF)

## Feelings — the rule people forget

Default for kids: **big feelings go to people first.** A parent, a grandparent, a school counselor, a coach. An AI is okay for little feelings ("I'm nervous about the test") but not the point where big feelings should end up — and a chat log of a kid's inner life is a permanent, reviewable, downloadable record.

Also for everyone: an AI is *not a diary*. "I'll never tell anyone this" is not how chat logs work. If your kid types it, treat it as published.

## A short family script

> "If it's information about me, someone I love, or how I really feel — that goes to a person, not to the app. If it's a picture of my sandwich or homework I want explained — that's fine."

## Special note: the image generators

Image tools are where kids get creative AND into trouble. Three things worth saying at your table:

1. No real person's face in a generation. Deepfakes of classmates are a school-discipline issue now, not just a mean joke.
2. Whatever it generates isn't their property in every tool — tools differ — so don't sell, claim, or submit generated art as their own ([homework honesty](02-homework-honesty-and-school-policies.html)).
3. If a generation creates something ugly, gross, or scary, that content stays in the account's history; bring it to a parent instead of hiding it.

## One-line takeaway

**If it's personal — a face, a last name, a place, a big feeling — it doesn't go to the AI. It goes to a person.**
