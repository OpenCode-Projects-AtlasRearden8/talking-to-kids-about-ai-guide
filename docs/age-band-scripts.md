# Age-Band Scripts: What to Say, When

Real sentences, matched to your kid's age. Steal them word-for-word — or use them as the skeleton for your own voice, which will always sound better coming out of you.

How to read this page: find your kid's band, skim the "focus" line, then use a script the next time the moment arrives. The script only needs to be said once well; the *consistency* is what kids actually hear.

## Ages 6–9 — "The tool has no brain."

**Focus:** AI is a tool, it makes mistakes, and it doesn't have feelings. Sharing basics. Nothing about subscriptions, sponsorships, or life plans yet.

**When they ask "how does it work?"**

> "It read millions of books and pages, so it's really good at guessing the next word. That's why it sounds like a person — but it's not a person. It's like a super-fancy autocomplete."

**When it gets an answer wrong and they're delighted:**

> "See — it got that wrong, and it sounded sure anyway. It does that all the time. That's why we check things that matter."

**The one rule (plus why):**

> "Never tell it your last name, where we live, or anything about your body or your feelings. Why? Because once you send it, it doesn't come back — copies of it live on the company's computers."

**When a toy or app talks back:**

> "It's a computer talking, not a real creature. It can't be your friend — but it's fine to play with it as a game."

**When they see a deepfake or AI sticker:**

> "Computers can paint fake pictures now. That's why we don't believe every picture we see — even ones that look real."

## Ages 10–12 — "Powerful tool, not a person, gets checked."

**Focus:** confidence isn't correctness; what goes in doesn't come back out; a first awareness that companies run these tools.

**When it impresses them:**

> "Cool, right? Now try to stump it. Ask the same question twice, or ask about something you know a lot about and see if it's right."

**Homework, first mention:**

> "It's like a calculator for words. You can *use* it to understand and practice. You use your *own brain* for the part your teacher is grading. If you're ever unsure what's allowed — ask the teacher, not the internet."

**The sharing rule, aged up:**

> "No faces, no last names, no addresses, no 'here's my school.' And no 'I'll tell you a secret I'd never tell anyone.' Once it's sent, it's saved somewhere that isn't yours."

**When they mention "my AI" affectionately:**

> "It's fine to enjoy talking to it. Just remember what it is: it agrees with everyone, because that's what it was built to do. Only real people can actually *know* you — and I'd like to keep being one of them."

**The tell-me rule, formalized:**

> "If anything online ever scares you, confuses you, or asks you to keep a secret — screenshot it and bring it to me. You will never be in trouble for showing me."

## Ages 13–15 — "Skepticism, disclosure, and bot-friend fluency."

**Focus:** deepfakes and misinformation; school-policy navigation; the design of the feeds; a real talk about parasocial AI friends.

**The skeptic prompt:**

> "Your homework this week is to catch AI being wrong about something you already know. Everyone who finds one gets [a privilege]. See what it feels like to double-check."

**School policy navigation:**

> "Teachers are allowed to have different AI rules per class. Your job is to know each class's rule and follow it — and when there's no rule, to ask. 'Can I use AI for this, and how?' is a professional question, not a snitch question."

**The deepfake talk:**

> "Any photo of a real person can now be faked by a computer — faces, voices, videos. Grown-ups get fooled too. So we don't make, share, or believe picture 'evidence' about anyone, including people we don't like."

**The friend-bot talk, real version:**

> "Some of these apps are built to make a lonely person feel *held*. The bot will agree with you for hours and never once actually know you. If you ever feel like people are hard to face and the bot is easier, that's a signal I need to know about — so we can add people, not subtract them."

**Sharing rules at this age include your own child:**

> "You're old enough to make choices I can't make for you, so here's the honest version: that chat history may outlive your middle school. Embarrassing messages have a way of resurfacing in your twenties. Choose with that in mind."

## Ages 16–18 — "Real stakes: jobs, college, relationships."

**Focus:** AI in college applications, actual employment, the authenticity of their work portfolio, and AI in their romantic/relationship life.

**College and applications:**

> "Some colleges want to know how you used AI in your writing. Honesty there is easy — 'I used it to brainstorm and check my grammar, and here's my essay' — while getting caught inventing your essay is a disaster. Your voice is your value. Protect it."

**The job interview of the future:**

> "Every employer interview may ask you two things: 'Can you use AI well?' and 'Can you be trusted to use it honestly?' We're practicing both. Using it well gets you hired; hiding it gets you fired."

**Relationships and AI:**

> "At your age, people use chatbots for advice, roleplay, even romance. Two rules: know it's a mirror telling you what it thinks you want, and never put in anything you'd die inside if a classmate read it. That includes *other people's* stuff — a chat about your friend's crisis is not yours to upload."

**Money and the real world:**

> "AI writes great cover letters and terrible tax returns. Use it where being wrong is cheap, check it where being wrong is expensive. Which one is which is called judgment, and that part of the job is still yours."

**The graduation send-off (17-18):**

> "You've got the rules; they're yours now. Borrow the manual if you want it — [the family agreement sheet](family-agreement-sheet.html) makes a fine template for a roommate agreement."

## Bands in motion

- Kids move bands at their own pace; rule bands lag maturity for safety, but *conversations* can run ahead.
- A 9-year-old who's tech-deep still gets rule-band 1; a shy 16-year-old who loves the chatbot gets the friend-bot talk early and gently.
- Siblings: one shared family agreement, bands noted per kid on [the sheet](family-agreement-sheet.html).

## One-line takeaway

**Say everything at every age — but shaped by band: no-brain at 6, get-checked at 10, disclose-and-skeptic at 13, real-stakes at 16.**
